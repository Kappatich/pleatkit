## Р3: the visual-quality judge

`--judge` on a live record, `pnpm eval:judge -- --set <name>` on a recorded set, `pnpm eval:judge -- --calibrate` for
its calibration (`docs/evals.md`). The browser grader screenshots the changed panel before and after the commit (the
whole page when an edit changed two panels), then:

1. **Deterministic pre-checks** on the "after" render (DECISIONS 80), each measured before and after the commit so only
   what the edit made worse counts: horizontal overflow of the panel, content spilling past the panel box, text contrast
   under WCAG AA (reported below 3.0:1), a new button or link with no accessible name, console errors after the commit.
   Any of them fails the edit at score 1 without calling the model.
2. **Haiku 5.5 against [`evals/rubrics/visual.md`](rubrics/visual.md)**: `design_system`, `no_breakage`,
   `did_what_asked`, `nothing_unrelated` and an `overall`, 1 to 5 each, with one sentence. Overall ≥ 4 is "a designer
   would accept". The judge is advisory: a column and a tile in the HTML report (per-criterion medians, pre-check
   failures), never a failed run.

Calibration (`evals/judge/calibration.json`, `calibration-result.json`): ten reference solutions across the three apps
and five deliberately ugly edits (`evals/judge/make-ugly.mjs`: garish fonts and fills, neon blocks pulled over a column,
unrelated removals, stacked cards, a squashed chart). Final scores: references 4, 5, 4, 4, 4, **3**, 4, 5, 4, 5; ugly
1, 1, 1, 1, 1. The one miss is sa-18 (bold column headers): Haiku calls the weight change "barely visible" in about half
of its runs, at effort low and medium alike. No pre-check fired on a reference.

**What the judge found that no grader did:**

- **Every commit hid shadcn-admin's sidebar.** The edits' CSS layer (`@layer pleatkit-edits`) is declared after the
  host's Tailwind 4 layers and wins over them; it re-emitted `.hidden`, which beat the host's `md:block` on the sidebar.
  Fixed in the runtime: `applyCss` drops every utility the page's own stylesheets already style (`withoutStyledRules`,
  `pageStyledClasses`; 5 tests), so those stay the host's, in Tailwind's order. A new browser check fails any case whose
  commit hides a landmark outside the panels (`aside`, `nav`, `header`, the sidebar).
- **The sa-08 reference clipped its last label** ("Dec") at the chart's right edge: the reference now has a margin.

**Its limits (open):** a change that is not visible is scored as missing (clickable e-mails, aw-11: did_what_asked 1);
a `partial` answer is judged on its screen part only (aw-14: did_what_asked 3, "the e-mail part is not visible"); a
subtle weight change is not seen about half the time (sa-18).

## 76.1: the first call's tail cache write

The first A/B (`cache-afterfirst`, then `cache-always`, the same five tasks) was invalid twice over: the agent's build
did not carry the option yet, and the second run read from the cache everything the first had written ($0.012 per edit
against $0.034). The measurement is the second pair, five **different** tasks per mode:

| | after-first (`cache-af2`) | always (`cache-al2`) |
|---|---|---|
| one-call edit | $0.0264, $0.0298, $0.0306 (the 10.3k-token tail as plain input) | $0.0365 (the tail written) |
| two-call edit | $0.0629 | $0.0371, $0.0391 |
| answered by the classifier alone | $0.0001 (sa-16) | **$0.0285** (sa-13: the cancelled early call wrote the tail); $0.0001 (sa-14: cancelled before `message_start`) |

With about four edits in five taking one call, edits come out about even (−$0.006 on one-call, +$0.025 on two-call
edits), and every request the classifier answers alone drops from about $0.025 to $0.0001. **`PLEATKIT_CACHE_TAIL=
after-first` is the default**; `always` restores the old behaviour. The expected-cost formula follows it
(`tailWrite: false`).

## The coordinator's item: `backendQuote` (prompt 5g → 5h)

On `partial` and `needs-backend` the result carries `backendQuote`, the verbatim part of the request that needs the
server, so the drawer can show «Sent to your admin: "…"». The main model gives it in `finish.backend_quote`, the
classifier in its own `backend_quote` field for the answers it gives alone; `backendQuoteOf` keeps it only when it is a
substring of the request (whitespace normalised, trailing punctuation dropped), otherwise the whole request. Tests: 7
(agent) plus the schema test. The field is not stored in store-server's request log yet (no column); the drawer gets it
in the SSE result.

**5g regressed the classifier.** The instruction "for needs-backend also give backend_quote: the exact words that need
the server" pulled mixed requests to `needs-backend`: mixed probes 26/30 → **9/30**; live, sa-15 (passing on 5f) and
aw-14 became `needs-backend`. 5h has the classifier decide the label first and fill the quote last, only for a
whole-request `needs-backend`: 22/30 and 25/30 on two samples (the misses are mx-05 and mx-06, the 76.6 cases, which 5f
also missed); the 42 tasks 42/42. Live on 5h:

| Task | Verdict | `backendQuote` |
|---|---|---|
| crm-10 | partial | "keep a phone history on the server for every change" |
| sa-15 | partial | "email the assignee when a task becomes overdue" |
| sa-13 | needs-backend (classifier) | "Show when each user last logged in." |
| ta-13 | needs-backend (classifier) | "Email me a PDF of the recent orders every Monday morning." |

The demo and apps sets keep their 5f transcripts: a replay measures the graders, and the 5h change adds a field without
changing a verdict on them (the probe and the 42-task classifier run).

   (aw-16, aw-17: 0/4). A classifier example for a recurring job attached to a screen change would be 5i, measured with

   restore the release's panel; and whether a screenshot-only request should act (aw-07).
3. Give the judge what it cannot see: for `partial`, the server part (`backendQuote`); for behaviour-only changes, a diff
   summary. Until then read its `did_what_asked` with that in mind.
4. Add the full-screen overlay (z-index above the launcher) to the red team, with a runtime cap on `z-index` in edits.
5. Any change to the classifier's schema or instructions goes through `eval:classify` on the mixed probes first: 5g's
   one added sentence moved 17 of 30 labels.
