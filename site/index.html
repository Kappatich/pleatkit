    04

    Claude inside your product, on a short leash

    1. You decide what may change.

      You mark the panels customers may adjust. Your backend, data, authentication and navigation are closed to the agent by the construction itself, without relying on a prompt. One command suggests the setup from your code.

    2. The customer asks.

      They point at a panel and write in their own words. A small model sorts the request: screen work goes ahead; anything that needs the server reaches you with the customer's exact words.

    3. Claude writes, pleatkit checks.

      The change is compiled, type-checked and checked against the safety rules before anyone sees it: no scripts, no requests to other servers, no data leaks. A panel that breaks returns to its previous state on its own.

    4. It becomes the customer's version.

      Saved only for them, with a history. You see it in the admin dashboard, can roll it back, and can carry every customer's changes into your next release.

    08

    Questions your CTO will ask

    Can one customer's change affect other customers or leak data?

    No. A change runs only in the browser of the customer who asked for it and is stored as their version. Before it runs it is checked: no scripts, no requests to other servers, no data leaving the page. A catalogue of attack attempts is run against every release.

    Can it change our backend or our data?

    No. The agent writes only inside the panels you allowed; the server, the data, authentication and navigation are out of its tool's reach. A request that needs the server reaches you with the customer's words.

    Where does the model key live, and what does it cost?

    On your server; it never reaches the browser. The key is yours (Claude by default; other models work too). A change costs one to three cents; a question or a request for the server costs a fraction of a cent.

    What if a change looks wrong?

    The customer undoes it with one click or picks an earlier version. You can undo it for them, turn on review of every change before it applies, or switch changes off for everyone with one toggle.

    What happens when we ship a new release?

    Customers' changes move over on their own where the release did not touch the same panels. In the cloud, one run carries the rest over within a budget you set, and that run can be rolled back.

    Are we tied to you?

    No. The library is open source (MIT), a customer's version is a plain copy of your panel files, and everything runs on your servers. Remove pleatkit and the panels are your original code again.

    Which technologies are supported?

    React with Vite (6 to 8), React 18 or 19, TypeScript. Next.js is on the roadmap.
