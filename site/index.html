    02

    Claude inside your product, on a leash

    1. You decide what may change.

      Mark the panels customers may customise. Your backend, data layer, auth and navigation stay closed, by construction, not by a prompt. One command suggests the setup from your code.

    2. The customer asks.

      They point at a panel and write what they want. A small model sorts the request: screen work goes ahead, anything that needs the server goes to you instead, with the exact words.

    3. Claude writes, pleatkit checks.

      The change is compiled, type-checked and scanned before anyone sees it: no scripts, no requests to other servers, no data leaving the page. A panel that breaks rolls itself back.

    4. It becomes their version.

      Saved for that customer with undo and a history. You see it in the admin dashboard, can roll it back, and carry everyone's changes over to your next release.

    06

    Questions your CTO will ask

    Can a customer's change affect other customers or leak data?

    No. A change runs only in the browser of the customer who asked for it and is stored as their version. Before it runs, it is compiled, type-checked and scanned: no scripts, no requests to other servers, no data leaving the page, nothing outside the allowed panels. We test these rules against a catalogue of attacks on every release.

    Can it change our backend or our data?

    It cannot. The agent can only write inside the panels you allow; the server, the data layer, authentication and navigation are not reachable by the tool itself. A request that needs the backend is routed to you with the customer's words instead of being faked.

    Where does the model key live and what does it cost?

    On your own agent server, never in the browser. You bring your own key (Claude by default; OpenAI-compatible endpoints work too). A change costs one to three cents; a question or a request that needs your backend costs a fraction of a cent.

    What if a change looks wrong?

    The customer undoes it with one click, or picks an earlier version from the history. You can undo it for them from the dashboard, review every change before it applies, or switch changes off for everyone with one toggle.

    What happens when we ship a new release?

    Customers' changes are carried over automatically where the release did not touch the same panels; the rest are migrated by the agent within a budget you set, and you can roll a release back.

    Are we locked in?

    No. The library is MIT, a customer's version is a plain copy of your panel files, and everything runs on your own servers if you want. Remove pleatkit and your panels are your original code again.

    Which stacks?

    React with Vite (6 to 8), React 18 or 19, TypeScript. Next.js is on the roadmap. Claude Sonnet 5.5 writes changes and Haiku 5.5 sorts requests by default; cheaper models are documented in the repository.
